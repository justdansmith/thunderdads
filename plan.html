Where things stand

  • About $3,000 to $4,000 sits in the Thunder Dads account, and this month's Kids Night Out could bring it near $5,000.
  • Principal LaShante Scott has asked the dads for a plan to spend it this school year.
  • The PFC spends each year's money that year. Only the auction and the "fund in need" (I think that's the auction's Fund-a-Need) carry over.
  • The dads want to decide where their money goes, and Selam asks each month for someone to give a dads update at the PFC.
  • Kids Night Out: I paid $35 for a first kid and about $30 for a second. Sign-in took 20 to 30 minutes with a long line, and the pizza ran out.
  • Goal for the year: $10,000, with the turf area as this year's big project.
  • Other projects on the table: desk and chair repairs, the picket fence, shade for the yard, and the music teacher's request for folding stage walls at $7,000 to $10,000.

Five ideas

01

Pay-what-you-can tickets and a movie vote

Givebutter can't let a buyer type their own ticket price, but it allows unlimited ticket types and an optional donation at checkout. A sliding scale of fixed tiers (pay it forward, standard, community, pay what you can, volunteer) gets the same result.

The movie vote fits on the ticket too: a dropdown question with three movies, tallied from the ticket export. One catch: showing a movie outside the home needs a public performance license even at a free event, so we should check whether the school or OUSD already holds one.

Details for whoever owns Kids Night Out: the Kids Night Out plan page, which stands on its own.

02

Fix-it projects and this year's turf project

  • Clearance. OUSD's supervised tier needs registration, TB clearance, and mandated reporter training with an OUSD employee present. A Saturday workday with no staff falls in the unsupervised tier, which adds Live Scan fingerprints and a waiver. Ed Fund isn't covering fingerprint costs this year.
  • Turf is the big one. Start with what kind (synthetic or grass), where it goes, and one installer quote. Shade would be district construction overseen by California's Division of the State Architect; I haven't confirmed whether turf needs the same review, so that's a question for the principal. Gifts to OUSD go through a board acceptance vote; the board accepted a $5,000 Crocker Highlands PTA donation that way in 2013.
  • If it's synthetic, ask installers for PFAS-free turf. A 2023 state bill banning PFAS turf at schools was vetoed, and I haven't found a newer rule.
  • The money clock. Under the spend-it-this-year rule the dads can't bank money across years. If turf costs more than this year's money, making it the auction's Fund-a-Need gets around that, because that line carries over.

Next: a core crew cleared now, and a yard walk plus one installer quote, which turns the turf idea into a number the PFC can vote on.

03

A Thunder Dads site with a request flow

The site draft follows the Montclair Dads Club page (why, what we do, how to join) and adds what Montclair's page lacks: the $10,000 goal as a thermometer, a request form for teachers, a project board anyone can read, and a list of projects finished this year. Behind it sits a Google Sheet, so whoever runs Thunder Dads next can keep it going without code.

  • Big project: the year's main fundraising goal. This year, the turf area.
  • Fix it: dad labor plus small materials from the fix-it fund.
  • Fund it: a bigger materials buy, under a cap the dads set.
  • Find it: beyond the dads' budget, where the dads help find the money.

The stage walls are the first Find it case. Start by asking the music teacher what the walls need to do (hide backstage, control sound, a backdrop, storage), since that drives the price. Then the routes: DonorsChoose if the teacher works at least 0.75 of a full-time position (it allows classroom furniture; I couldn't confirm stage equipment), the auction's Fund-a-Need, the PFC budget, or an OUSD facilities request if anything attaches to the building.

Next: agree on the lanes and a per-project cap, then I build the live version.

04

Faster sign-in, safer pickup

Givebutter's free app already scans each ticket's QR code and shows the answers collected at checkout, so required ticket questions plus two or three scanners fix the line. Pickup needs its own record: matching wristband numbers and a one-screen Google Form log with a timestamp on every release.

Next: the Kids Night Out questions for the principal, listed below.

05

A monthly PFC update, and request alerts on WhatsApp

A one-page update for the PFC each month: the thermometer, money in, money out, and the project board counts. For alerts, WhatsApp doesn't let an ordinary account post into a group from code. Meta's group messaging for businesses reportedly requires an officially verified business account and small groups (vendor-reported; I couldn't confirm on Meta's pages), and third-party tools that post through a personal number break WhatsApp's rules.

  • Recommended: each form submission emails the coordinators a summary with a one-tap WhatsApp link, message already written. A coordinator picks the chat and sends. Free, and within the rules.
  • Fully automatic, to individuals: CallMeBot, free for personal use, messages each coordinator's own phone. It can't post to the group.

Next: add a "Fix-it requests" group to the community so requests have their own channel.

Questions for the principal

  1. Do parents watching kids at Kids Night Out count as unsupervised OUSD volunteers?

    If so, every free volunteer ticket needs fingerprints and a badge.

  2. Who is the responsible adult on record for Kids Night Out?

  3. Does the PFC's insurance cover the event?

  4. Does the school or OUSD hold a movie license that covers Kids Night Out?

    Licenses are per school site, and districts sometimes already hold one.

  5. Can we walk the yard about a turf area, and what approval does turf need?

  6. Would folding stage walls attach to the building?

    If they do, they go through OUSD facilities.

Decisions for the dads

  • Synthetic or grass for the turf area, and where it goes
  • Ticket tiers and the lowest price, once someone knows what a kid costs in pizza and supplies
  • Who owns Kids Night Out tickets and pickup, and the three movies for the first vote
  • The per-project cap for Fund it, and who sorts new requests each week

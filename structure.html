  • The club decides spending together, by a clear process. Jason, Scott, Dilip

Every model keeps us inside the PFC

Our money already sits in the PFC's account, and the PFC Treasurer collects money, pays expenses, and reports finances at every PFC meeting. A separate 501(c)(3) would mean our own bank account, yearly IRS and state filings, and officers of record. Jason is asking the Montclair Elementary dads club how they handle it.

Pick one, or mix parts. Dollar limits and anything marked Proposal are starting points for the club to set. The BAC's public page covers its roles, working groups, meetings, and school ties; we've asked Jon how it handles terms and money.

How a request gets decided

Every request answers six questions

  1. What is it, and who does it help?
  2. How much, and what does it buy?
  3. When is the money needed?
  4. Will dads do any of the work?
  5. Who is asking, and who owns it?
  6. Which area: running costs, small fixes, big projects, or outside the plan?

A good project for us

  • Helps Thornhill kids or teachers directly.
  • Uses dads' time or skills, beyond money.
  • Can be finished or funded this school year.
  • Has the principal on board, with any OUSD approval known.

One decision log

Date, request, amount, decision, vote, owner, receipt filed. The Secretary keeps it, or a co-chair in the BAC model.

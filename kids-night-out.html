Three changes for the next event: a pay-what-you-can ticket option with a movie vote, a sign-in line that moves, and a pickup log that protects the dads. Everything here is a proposal for you to change.

Pay-what-you-can tickets

Givebutter has no ticket where the buyer types their own price. Its help center lists price as a fixed setting on each ticket type: "The price of the ticket, or tick the checkbox if this is a free ticket." It does allow unlimited ticket types and an optional donation step at checkout, and those two cover the idea.

Prices are a proposal. Recent tickets ran about $35 for a first kid and $30 for a second. Confirm the current price and the cost per kid before setting tiers.

Keep the checkout donation on

Givebutter's checkout "presents tickets first, with the option to add a donation." A family on a low tier can still add a few dollars.

Optional: a quiet discount code

Promo codes can take a percentage or a fixed amount off each ticket and can be limited to specific tickets. A code shared through teachers covers families who'd rather not pick a low tier in public.

Givebutter's "registration fundraising" does let people type their own amount above a minimum, but it allows one person per checkout and is built for walkathon-style fundraisers. It's the wrong fit for a parent with two kids.

Givebutter setup

  1. Add one ticket type per tier

    Price is fixed per ticket type. Tick the free checkbox for the Volunteer ticket.

  2. Add required questions to every ticket

    Ticket custom fields can be text, number, checkbox, option, or file, with a "Required field" toggle and an "Apply to all tickets" setting. Ask for: child's name, grade, allergies or medical notes, and the adults allowed to pick up, with a phone number.

  3. Add the movie vote

    An option field with three movies, single select. Custom field answers come through in the ticket export, so the tally takes a minute. Confirm the movie license first (see the questions below).

  4. Run a free test order with two tickets

    Givebutter's docs don't say outright that a two-kid order asks the questions once per ticket. A test order settles it before families start buying.

  5. Export the list the day before

    The ticket details export includes the custom field answers. Print it by last name as the backup if the app or Wi-Fi fails.

Sign-in that moves

Last time the sign-in line ran 20 to 30 minutes. With every answer collected at checkout, the door only has to scan and hand out a wristband.

Family arrives→Scan the ticket QR code→Wristband on the kid, matching stub to the parent→In

Two or three scanners

Every ticket is emailed with a QR code, and the free Givebutter app scans it. The check-in screen shows the answers collected at checkout.

A separate help desk

Walk-ups, lost tickets, and changes go to one side table so the main line keeps moving. Manual lookup works by name, email, or ticket number.

Check the Wi-Fi first

Givebutter recommends confirming Wi-Fi at the venue and having a manual backup. The printed export is that backup.

Pickup with a record

Pickup is where the liability sits. Givebutter's check-in has one state and no sign-out, and un-checking someone only flips the status back, so it can't serve as a pickup log.

Parent shows the stub→Number matches the wristband→Adult is on the pickup list→Logged on the tablet→Out

Matching numbers

A double roll of raffle tickets does the job: one number on the kid's wristband, the matching stub with the parent. The pickup list lives in each ticket's answers in the Givebutter app.

A one-screen pickup log

A Google Form on a tablet: kid's name, picked up by, volunteer's initials. Every release gets a timestamp in a Google Sheet, and that record protects the dads if a pickup is ever questioned.

Build an app later, if needed. If pickup turns out to be the bottleneck, a small app that scans the ticket QR code to sign a kid out is a weekend build. Dan has offered to build it.

Questions to settle first

These decide how the free volunteer ticket works. Ask the principal and the PFC before the next event.

  1. Do parents watching kids count as unsupervised OUSD volunteers?

    OUSD's unsupervised tier needs fingerprints, a TB clearance, and a signed waiver, and comes with a green badge. Supervised volunteers need an OUSD employee present.

  2. Who is the responsible adult on record for the evening?

  3. Does the PFC's insurance cover Kids Night Out?

  4. Does the school or OUSD hold a movie license that covers Kids Night Out?

    Showing a film outside the home needs a public performance license, even when the event is free, and owning the DVD doesn't count. Swank Movie Licensing USA covers the major studios. Licenses are per school site, and districts sometimes already hold one.

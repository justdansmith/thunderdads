Decisions

For the December vote

We agreed on the direction. The full club makes it official.

  • Two co-chairs instead of a President and Vice President. Staggered two-year terms, like the PFC co-presidents, so a new co-chair always learns from one in their second year. Both co-chairs sign off on spending, and they're our PFC liaison.
  • A Treasurer who keeps our books. Ali offered. Money in, money out, and what's coming, every month or two. The PFC keeps the bank account.
  • A lead for every event. The lead doesn't do everything; they make sure everything gets done, with a playbook anyone can pick up.
  • Up to $100, buy it for a project and file the receipt. Over $100, check with the co-chairs first. A proposal to vote on in December, with the rest of the money rules.
  • Regular meetings on a set day, monthly or every other month. Elections get a few weeks' notice, with a way to vote by proxy.
  • Thunder Dads stays the name, and everyone is welcome. Dads, father figures, and every caregiver.
  • Text-only shirts for now. The logo is on hold while the school reviews it, so we use words only, with a new design each year.
  • No separate nonprofit. We stay inside the PFC, which holds our money, and keep it simple.

Leaning

  • Staff Safety Team supplies (lanyards, flashlights, games): leaning no. A purchase with no dad work, outside our projects. We answer once the mission is written, so the reason is clear.

Still open: is it Thunder Dads or ThunderDads? The site says two words; the teachers' request form says one.

What we talked about

Turf has its own momentum.

A donor has offered to fund turf, and Malcolm and Deepa are in talks with the principal, the JCC, and America SCORES. Malcolm owns it for us. America SCORES has installed turf at other schools and would run its after-school soccer program here. At Montclair, turf cost about $45,000 in materials with volunteer labor, glued right on top of the blacktop. Placement idea: centered in the play area, which may mean moving the ball cage.

Shade is ours to lead.

A permanent 16 by 20 foot canopy came in around $40,000, most of it engineering and accessibility review. Retractable awnings bolted to a portable cost about $4,000 each, reach 14 feet wide and 13 feet out, and dads can install them. A church's set has lasted 15 to 18 years. The principal wants shade on every portable: two per 41-foot portable, eight in all, about $32,000. We start with one, placed by the sun pattern.

Light for evening play.

After the time change the yard is dark by 5, and after-care is still outside. About five 1,000-watt LED lights could light the whole yard, roughly $1,500 to $2,000. It goes on the idea list.

We pick our projects.

We partner with the principal, the PFC, and the other school groups, and say yes to work that fits what we're about: projects we believe in, where dads' hands matter. Now that Kids Night Out makes money, more requests will come, so we need a mission to point to and a top project we're saving for. One idea: hold 10 to 15 percent of the money for outside requests.

Show where the money goes.

Families give more when they know what they're paying for. A theme for the year, like kids' health and play, with turf and shade under it, makes that easy. Events that give families real value raise the most: Kids Night Out is three hours of childcare for $35. A bike rodeo could work the same way. We line our project fundraising up with the PFC so it helps the auction, for example by running it after March 20.

How the BAC handles money.

A past BAC co-chair told Scott their budget comes through the PFC and both co-chairs sign off on spending. No board vote for routine buys. A quorum and a few weeks' notice only for big decisions like electing a co-chair.

What Montclair's dads club does.

Ten or more regulars at every meeting. Every role is two years, and the second year trains the next person. A home brew fest four or five times a year: about 300 guests at $65 a ticket. A mystery night at about $150 a ticket that brings in around $6,000. Year-end leftover money covers the fifth-grade trip's usual $3,000 to $4,000 gap. Their dads club runs events under the PTA's insurance through Facilitron instead of paying about $500 each time.

Kids Night Out keeps getting better.

December 4 is booked. Givebutter's QR codes replace the paper list: two dads scan at the door, and pickup needs the same code. We break even at about 31 kids; October had almost 80. Every job gets a name: pizza order and pickup, popcorn, door, and an MC.

Recruiting works face to face.

Handing out notices at school on the Monday of Kids Night Out week took signups from 27 to 75 in two days. Dads say yes when another dad asks them directly. A short list of what we've already done shows new dads it's real. A free first beer at meetings could help recruiting, if we say so up front and keep it small, about $100.

The social side matters.

Plenty of men won't go out just to socialize, but they'll show up for a purpose and a team. Many hands make the work light, and if it isn't fun, nobody comes back.

Requests need a simple front door.

Teachers already have a request form, but many don't know it. Planned workdays with one compiled list work better than one-off asks. A posted cost line helps people sort themselves: a $10,000 stage project won't fit this year.

Getting shirts made

  • Cotton tees made in the Philippines now owe about 29 percent in duties: 16.5 percent base plus a 12.5 percent tariff since July 2026, according to trade-news sources. The duty-free allowance for small shipments ended in August 2025. A $4 shirt lands above $5 before shipping and broker fees. Source
  • Bonfire runs free, print-on-demand stores with no inventory; we set the price and keep the profit. Source
  • Spirit Hero starts with a flash sale, 36 shirts per design, then keeps the store open year-round with no minimums. Source
  • Custom Ink Fundraising is free to run, but each campaign has a print minimum, and buyers get refunds if it isn't met. Source

What it means for us: a print-on-demand store for everyday sales, and one bulk order for volunteer shirts.

Research

Topics from the meeting, looked up so we can decide with facts. Anything we couldn't confirm says so.

An awning on a portable needs sign-off

  • OUSD: no one does any work at a school site without prior approval from Facilities, Buildings and Grounds. Volunteer projects go through OUSD's Volunteer Facility Improvement Projects form. Source
  • State: small alterations to school buildings, up to $134,000, skip the State Architect's plan review, but they still need drawings from a licensed design professional and a state-certified inspector. Source
  • The State Architect's exempt list covers shading that needs no structural attachment. An awning bolted to the building isn't on it. Source
  • Free-standing shade has its own rules: open on the sides, at least 5 feet from a building, fire-retardant fabric. Source
  • Shade works: in a 2022 playground study, shaded surfaces never got hot enough to burn. Source

What it means for us: Ask Buildings and Grounds before we buy the first awning, and budget for an engineer's drawing and an inspection on top of the $4,000.

Turf runs hot, so pair it with shade

  • America SCORES lists Thornhill among its Oakland partner schools. In 2024 its Field of Dreams program replaced asphalt with 35,000 square feet of turf at Brookfield Elementary. Its contact for school partnerships is Chief Program Officer Yuri Morales. Source
  • In a study cited by New York State's health department, synthetic turf averaged 117°F in the sun, asphalt 110°F, and grass 78°F. Watering cooled turf for only about 20 minutes. Source
  • California has no ban on PFAS in turf: a 2023 bill was vetoed and a 2025 bill failed in February 2026. San Francisco now tests new turf for PFAS. Source
  • New turf play areas skip the State Architect's structural review but still get an accessibility review. Source

What it means for us: Ask the partners for PFAS-free turf with test results, and offer a dads crew for install day.

Lights without new wiring

  • New light fixtures and circuits count as an alteration: licensed design and a state-certified inspector. OUSD treats added power for new equipment as new work that needs a request form. Source
  • Battery tower lights need no wiring: Milwaukee's 27,000-lumen tower light lists at $3,999 and runs about 10 hours on a charge; a 15,000-lumen model was $1,899 on sale. Source
  • Solar trailer light towers cost $21,000 to $29,000 to buy, or $100 to $210 a day to rent. Source

What it means for us: The $1,500 to $2,000 floodlight idea grows once permits come in. One or two battery towers that roll out of storage could light after-care this winter, with no district electrical work.

Insurance for our events

  • OUSD books every facility use through Facilitron and requires $1,000,000 per occurrence and $2,000,000 aggregate in general liability. Alcohol is prohibited. Source
  • The California State PTA's insurance doesn't cover parent clubs that aren't PTAs, so Montclair's route through its PTA isn't open to a PFC. Source
  • Parent-club policies like PTO Today's cover volunteers at events the club approves and mostly staffs, for $472 to $868 a year, and exclude events at private homes. Source
  • One-day event insurance usually runs $75 to $200. Source

What it means for us: Ask the PFC whether its own policy covers Thunder Dads events and can serve as the Facilitron certificate. If it can, every event gets cheaper.

Fundraisers with beer

  • Alcohol is prohibited on OUSD property, so any beer event happens off campus. Source
  • Homemade beer can't be sold in California. It can be donated to a nonprofit for a licensed fundraiser, drunk on site, and labeled as homemade. Source
  • A sale is any exchange of alcohol for money, so a ticket that includes beer most likely counts. That's our reading of the definition; no ABC page we found says it outright. Source
  • A nonprofit's daily beer and wine license costs $50, applied for 10 to 30 days ahead on form ABC-221, with a certified responsible beverage server on site the whole time. Source

What it means for us: A brew fest is doable, but it runs through the PFC as the licensed nonprofit, with its own insurance check for a private home.

Ryan's idea: open the playground on weekends

Ryan shared an idea for funding to open the playground on weekends; Dan caught up with him on the drive home. That part isn't on the recording, so the details are Ryan's to fill in.

How others have done it

  • San Francisco opened about 45 schoolyards on weekends starting in 2007. By 2022 it cost about $600,000 a year in custodial overtime, repairs, and liability. The district ended the program July 1, 2025 over cost; the yards are still open to groups by permit. Source
  • OUSD's 2019 Living Schoolyard policy says schools can serve as community resources after hours and on weekends through a joint use agreement. Source
  • The citywide OUSD and City of Oakland joint use agreement expired in 2013, according to a 2022 Aspen Institute report. Source
  • Kaiser Permanente gave $2.5 million through the East Bay Community Foundation to rebuild Oakland schoolyards. That money goes to construction, not weekend hours. Source
  • A state parks grant covers joint-use schoolyards that are open to the community after hours, but the city applies, not the school district. Source

What a weekend opening takes

  • Someone to unlock and lock the gates each day.
  • Cleanup, so the yard is ready Monday morning.
  • Restrooms, or a plan without them. OUSD can charge extra for restrooms and cleanup. Source
  • A permit through Facilitron, with $1,000,000 and $2,000,000 in liability insurance and an adult supervising. Source
  • The principal's OK, and probably the district's.
